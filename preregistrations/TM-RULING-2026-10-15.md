# Ruling: construction of arm TM (token-matched T), and the D1/concordance finding

Date: 2026-10-15. Design authority for `LOCAL-B1B3-ADDENDUM-PREREG.md` (committed 33aa630).
State at ruling: retrieval complete, B1 (ON/OFF/T × 3 seeds) generating, nothing graded or read, TM not
generated. This ruling is outcome-blind.

---

## 1. Ruling: option (c), a one-clause substitution in the TM padding rule

**TM padding candidates = the Q-ON48 hits whose file is not in files(ON12), in Q-ON48 rank order.**
Everything else in §3 TM is unchanged: T's chunks first in T order, then candidates appended under the
greedy closest-match rule on rendered characters with target `len(format_pack(OFF))`, chunks never
truncated, one chunk per file, short instances logged.

Formally, for each gate-1-passing instance:

    cands_TM = [h for h in Q-ON48.hits if h.file ∉ files(ON12)]        # ordered by Q-ON48 rank

On the gate-1 set files(OFF12) ⊆ files(ON12), so this also excludes every OFF and T file. The registered
clause "rank ≥ 13 in Q-ON48 and file ∉ files(T)" is replaced by "file ∉ files(ON12)". No rank cutoff.

This is the agent's option (b) with the vestigial `rank ≥ 13` clause removed. §2 explains why neither
(a) nor the literal (b) is the right construction.

## 2. What TM is supposed to isolate

The addendum defines TM for one purpose: `(OFF − T) = (OFF − TM) + (TM − T)` with
`TM − T = Δ_tokens-at-fixed-content` and `OFF − TM = Δ_content-at-fixed-tokens` (§3). Prediction 3 reads
TM ≈ T as "what the depth account predicts at fixed content", and §11 states the construct in words:
"TM pads with ranks 13+ by design, so its padding is lower-ranked, other-file material. TM − T therefore
measures adding lower-ranked breadth at OFF's size."

So TM must satisfy two properties:

- **P1. T's content is held fixed** (T is a prefix of TM). Both (a) and (b) satisfy this.
- **P2. The padding is material that no B1 arm served**, so that `TM − T` is attributable to "more
  tokens of plausibly-relevant, beyond-ON material" and not to re-adding ON's own breadth. "Ranks 13+"
  was the registered proxy for "beyond ON's 12". §4.5 registered the check that this proxy relies on
  (Q-ON48 top-12 = Q-ON12) and registered "the number of TM padding files that also appear in
  files(ON)" as the quantity to watch. The check failed on 0/500; the quantity is 1006.

Property P2 is the construct; the rank cutoff was its implementation. When a registered check shows that
the implementation does not realise the construct, the construct governs. That is the standard reading of
a pre-registration, and §4.5 shows the author anticipated exactly this failure mode.

## 3. Why not (a): the literal rule builds a partial ON

From the provenance (read-only analysis, 478 gate-1 instances):

- 1006 of 3704 padding chunks (27%) come from files(ON12). **944 of those 1006 are byte-identical to
  the chunk ON served for that file.** So under (a), about 2.1 of the 7.75 padding slots per instance
  re-insert ON's own trimmed-away chunks, and the rest are new. TM becomes T + (a 30% sample of ON's
  non-T files) + (beyond-ON files).
- `TM − T` then mixes the file-trim component that B1 already measures as `T − ON` (in reverse) with the
  beyond-ON component it is meant to measure. `OFF − TM` loses its reading as "content at fixed tokens
  against padding orthogonal to ON". The decomposition stays arithmetically exact but the labels on its
  terms become wrong.
- The agent's suggestion of defining rank from the limit-12 list makes this worse, not better: ON12's
  non-T files first, then beyond-ON files, gives 3057/3629 padding slots from ON files, i.e. TM ≈ ON
  reordered plus about one file. B2 would then replicate B1 at $0 and answer nothing. Rejected.

## 4. Why not the literal (b): the rank cutoff has no meaning once files(ON12) is excluded

The registered `rank ≥ 13` was a proxy for "beyond ON12". With the explicit file exclusion in place, the
cutoff no longer tracks anything: Q-ON48 is a re-ranking over a 240-deep pool, and on average 3.1 of its
top 12 are files not in ON12 (max 9). Those are precisely the non-ON files the deeper ranking rates
highest. Keeping `rank ≥ 13` would skip them and pad with files it rates lower, for no construct reason.

The two constructions differ materially: they produce identical padding on only 11/478 instances.

| construction | TM/OFF chars (mean) | within ±10% | pads/instance | candidates/instance (mean, min) | ran out | pad rank in Q-ON48 (mean, median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) literal, as built | 0.9996 | 99.8% | 7.75 | 34.5, 5 | 2 | 17.2, 17 |
| (b) literal + file ∉ ON12 | 0.9999 | 99.8% | 7.60 | 32.0, 4 | 3 | 19.1, 19 |
| **(c) file ∉ ON12, no rank cutoff** | 0.9997 | 100% | 7.25 | 35.1, 7 | 1 (requests-5414) | 13.9, 14 |

Under (c), 1398/3466 padding slots (40%) sit at Q-ON48 ranks ≤ 12. This is a feature. Every one of
them is beyond ON's 12 *by the ranking that produced ON* (the limit-12 query), which is the ranking that
defines "lower-ranked" in §11. (c) pads T with the best beyond-ON material the retriever offers. That is
the more conservative test of the depth account: if `TM ≈ T` and `OFF > TM` survive padding with the
retriever's next-best files, the result cannot be dismissed as "padded with junk". Under (b) it could.

**Trade-off stated plainly.** (b) is the smaller textual edit (one added exclusion). (c) is the cleaner
construct (one substitution: the proxy is replaced by the thing it proxied) and has the better size
match and fewer short instances. Both are outcome-blind and logged. I rule (c) on construct validity. If
the owner prefers the minimal-edit reading of pre-registration, (b) is acceptable and the deviation text
below needs only the candidate line changed; (a) is not acceptable.

**2026-10-15 (D1 revisited): single daemon does not resolve TRIMMED-BREADTH D1; cause corrected.**
Subset 478/500 = 95.6% (two daemons: 94.6%), top-concordance 88.2% (88.6%), any-concordance 93.9%
(94.4%), 21 of the 22 violators shared. The cause is deterministic and inside the fusion backend at
4fab615: with `code_only`, dedup-ON fetches 5 × limit candidates and dedup-OFF 3 × limit, and the fused
ranking (weighted RRF over depth-truncated arm lists, plus a graph arm seeded from the fused prefix with
`cap = fetch`) is not prefix-stable in depth. ON is therefore one-per-file over a 60-deep ranking while
OFF is the top 12 of a 36-deep ranking, in one process. D1's attribution to two daemons is withdrawn as
the primary cause; cross-process nondeterminism is at most a residual. No retrieval is re-run and no
arm is changed. Disclosure wording is in `TM-RULING-2026-10-15.md` §7.

## 7. How the paper should treat the D1/concordance finding

**Revise the explanation: yes.** The two-daemon account is contradicted by a direct replication (one
process reproduces the subset rate, both concordance rates, and 21/22 of the violators). The paper should
state the real cause once, in the problem-setting section, and cite it from both places that currently
lean on "by construction":

> The dedup flag is not a pure post-filter. In the deployed retriever it also sets the candidate depth
> (5 × limit with dedup, 3 × limit without), and the fused ranking is not stable under that depth
> (weighted RRF over truncated arm lists plus a graph arm seeded from the fused prefix). ON is therefore
> one-chunk-per-file over a 60-deep ranking and OFF the top 12 of a 36-deep one. files(OFF) ⊆ files(ON)
> holds on 95.6% of instances in a single process (94.6% across two), and ON's chunk is the top OFF
> chunk of its file in 88% of shared files. The limit-48 query's top 12 reproduces the limit-12 query in
> 0/500 (dedup-ON) and 4/500 (dedup-OFF) instances, which shows the instability directly.

**Interpretation of the T decomposition: unchanged in identification, sharpened in wording.**

- `T − ON` is exact: T's chunks are ON's chunks byte-for-byte and files(T) ⊆ files(ON) on the analysed
  set. Nothing about concordance touches this term.
- `OFF − T` is "OFF's served within-file content versus ON's served chunk, at OFF's file set". The
  reading "OFF = T plus extra same-file chunks" holds for 93.9% of files and 358/478 instances; in the
  rest OFF's chunks for a file differ from ON's chunk rather than extend it. The paper should say
  "within-file depth as served" rather than "additional chunks on top of T", and report the nested-subset
  sensitivity. The verdict rule is unchanged; the sensitivity is secondary.
- The headline ON − OFF contrast is "the dedup flag as deployed", which bundles the one-per-file filter
  with the 5×/3× depth difference. Say so in one sentence; it is a disclosure, not a change in any claim.
  T is what separates file-trim from the rest; the depth-difference part of "the rest" is not separable
  in these data.

**Further pre-registered action: only the two items in the §12 text** (nested-subset sensitivity; §7.3
rank-not-score note). No re-query, no new arm, no change to any gate, estimand, prediction or verdict
rule. The retrieval stack is shut down and the GPU is committed to B1/B2; nothing here warrants touching
either.

**The TM proxy failure and the D1 failure have one root cause.** State that once. A reviewer who sees two
separate "the ranking surprised us" entries will weight them as two problems; they are one property of
RRF over truncated lists, found twice by registered checks that were designed to find it.
